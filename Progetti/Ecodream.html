Overview

"Ecodream has a lot of potential and a powerful actual concept, but it needs to be unlocked with a better user experience and a more powerful design!"

Ecodream is a sustainable brand that aims to produce eco-friendly bags and accessories while promoting environmental responsibility. Its strenghts lie in the message they want to communicate, in their local production and the uniqueness and quality of their products. However, the current e-commerce website has some usability and accessibility issues that make it difficult to navigate and use and the design needs to be improved to better reflect the brand's values. The goal of this project is to redesign the whole e-commerce experience without losing the essence of the brand.

01

Research

What I did

-- Heuristic and Accessibility Analysis

  • I conducted a heuristic evaluation based on Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics to identify usability issues affecting navigation, product discovery and overall user experience, across both desktop and mobile devices.
  • I evaluated the existing site across five usability dimensions: Learnability, Efficiency, Memorability, Errors and Satisfaction. Each dimension was rated based on observed behaviour and friction points found during the audit.

What I discovered

  1. 01

    Users can't orient themselves. No breadcrumbs, poor navigation, no search bar. They don't know where they are or how to find what they need.

  2. 02

    Navigation creates constant friction. Product listing with no filters and key pages hard to reach. Too many confusing steps.

  3. 03

    Calls to action are invisible or ambiguous. Clickable images with no indication, unclear CTAs. Users don't know what to do next.

  4. 04

    Information is scattered and hard to scan. Excessive unformatted text, important info buried in wrong pages, same content repeated across pages.

  5. 05

    The brand story doesn't build trust. Sustainability values are highly present but poorly communicated: not specific, not visible enough, not well organized.

-- Competitor analysis

I analysed direct and indirect competitors to understand how sustainable fashion brands organise product information, communicate their values and guide users through the shopping experience.

"I wanted to understand how competing brands address similar user needs and identify best practices that could be adapted to improve the EcoDream experience."

-- User survey

Before designing for users, I needed to understand them.
I ran a survey covering demographics, sustainability awareness, budget range and shopping preferences. I also asked specific questions about the existing Ecodream shop: filter availability, product information clarity and navigation and explored what actually stops users from completing a purchase.

-- User personas + journey

Based on the data collected, I defined 3 user personas to identify a clear target audience. For each persona, I mapped a user journey to simulate realistic purchase and navigation experiences on the existing site, highlighting the key friction points along the way.

02

Strategy

Ecodream doesn't sell essential goods. As the survey revealed, most users abandon their cart because the purchase feels unnecessary, navigation issues and lack of product information come second.


But that's exactly the point!
We can't control personal decisions or budgets, but we can make sure that a confusing navigation or poor communication are never the reason someone walks away.
Why would anyone spend time on a poorly organised site when the competition is just one click away? The goal of this strategy is to remove every obstacle that had nothing to do with the product itself.


Main friction points

  1. Confusing Information Architecture

  2. Poor Product and Information discoverability

  3. Content Hierarchy & Readability

Strategic decisions

  1. Improve the information architecture to make content easier to find.

  2. Improve product discoverability through better navigation, filtering and content organization.

  3. Use a stronger visual hierarchy to guide users through the experience.

-- Reorganising information architecture

The original information architecture was all over the place. Pages were tangled with no clear logic, you'd expect to find something in one place and either find it somewhere else entirely, or not find it at all. Before designing anything, the structure needed to make sense.

04

Prototype & Usability Test

After completing the high-fidelity designs, I built interactive prototypes for both desktop and mobile in Figma, connecting all screens and interactions to prepare for testing with real users.

The goal was to test the design decisions made in the previous phases with real users, before considering the project complete.



Desktop prototype

Mobile prototype

Participants

  • 7 partecipants
  • Interested in sustainability
  • Familiar with e-commerce websites
  • Mixed age range and backgrounds

Objectives

  • Evaluate product discoverability
  • Assess navigation clarity
  • Verify task completion efficiency
  • Identify remaining usability issues before finalizing the design

Test Details

  • Unmoderated usability test
  • Duration: 5–10 minutes
  • Device: Mobile prototype
  • Location: Remote
  • Tool: Maze

Methodology

Participants were asked to complete 4 tasks using the interactive prototype.
Their behaviour and feedback were recorded and analysed to identify pain points and areas of improvement.

The task

  1. 01

    Find information about EcoDream’s values and sustainability mission.

  2. 02

    Explore the shop.

  3. 03

    Find a specific product: "Emess oro".

  4. 04

    Add the product to the wishlist.



Each task was paired with an ease-of-completion rating, and the test closed with an overall experience evaluation to obtain both quantitative and qualitative insight.

The outcome

100%

Task completion across all 4 tasks

4.7/5

Overall experience rating

86%

Found navigation as expected

  • Every participant successfully found company information, explored the shop, located a specific product and added it to the wishlist.

  • Misclick rates were higher than expected, particularly when finding company information (77.9%) and searching for a specific product (63.9%), suggesting some improvements are needed.

  • The missing search bar was confirmed as a priority, almost every participant clicked on the search bar, even though it wasn't part of the prototype, confirming it as a priority addition.

  • Qualitative feedback was largely positive, with users describing the experience as "intuitive", "accessible" and "easy to navigate". A few noted the interaction felt occasionally "slow" or "stiff", but it's mostly a prototype issue.

05

Conclusion

The EcoDream redesign successfully addressed the main usability issues identified during the research phase. The final usability test validated the proposed solutions while also highlighting new opportunities for future iterations and improvements.

As with any digital product, design is an ongoing process of learning, testing and refinement.
